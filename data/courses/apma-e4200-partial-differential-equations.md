## Why This Course Matters

Not a major requirement — the Chemical Physics math requirement stops at ODEs — so this is
a deliberate addition. Three places it pays off, none of them "because the Schrödinger
equation is a PDE" (electronic structure is solved as a matrix eigenvalue problem, so
quantum mechanics does not actually depend on this course):

- **Poisson–Boltzmann and continuum solvation.** A genuine PDE, solved numerically as one,
  and one of the Friesner group's stated research areas.
- **Diffusion, Fokker–Planck, Smoluchowski.** Kinetics, Brownian dynamics, and anything
  time-dependent in solvent.
- **Score-based generative models.** The frontier of molecular generative design is written
  in the language of SDEs and the Fokker–Planck equation. This is the difference between
  reading those papers and only running the code — which matters precisely *because* the
  formal ML coursework was cut.

Taken as **APMA E4200** rather than MATH UN3028: same subject, same 3 points, fall term,
and the applied-math treatment sits closer to the numerical side of the material. Filed
under Mathematics, with MATH UN2015 and UN2030.
