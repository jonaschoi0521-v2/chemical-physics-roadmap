## Why This Course Matters

Moved out of freshman spring deliberately. ODEs are **not** a gate for CHEM GU4221
Quantum Chemistry I in Semester III, for three reasons:

- The exactly solvable cases in GU4221 are named ODE problems - harmonic oscillator is
  Hermite's equation, rigid rotor is Legendre's, the hydrogen radial part is Laguerre's -
  but courses at that level present those solutions rather than deriving them. Levine and
  McQuarrie tabulate the polynomials.
- UN2030 is a standard applied ODE course: first-order equations of special types, linear
  equations with constant and variable coefficients, systems, series and transform methods.
  It is not a special-functions or Sturm-Liouville course, so the overlap with quantum
  chemistry is thinner than the name suggests.
- GU4221's content - perturbation theory, angular momentum and spin, spin-spin interactions
  - is dominated by linear algebra, not ODEs. MATH UN2015 stays in Semester II for exactly
  that reason.

Semester IV placement is still ahead of everything that needs it: **APMA E4300** (Semester V)
covers initial- and boundary-value problems for ODEs. PHYS UN3003 Mechanics is concurrent,
which is workable since mechanics teaches the ODEs it needs as it goes.

**Prerequisites:** MATH UN1102 and UN1201, or the equivalent - satisfied by MATH UN1205
(Accelerated Multivariable), which replaced the UN1201/UN1202 pair.
