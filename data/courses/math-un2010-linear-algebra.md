## Why This Course Matters

Linear algebra is the load-bearing prerequisite for the whole back half of this
roadmap: PDE (Sem IV), Machine Learning (Sem V), the quantum half of Physical
Chemistry, Stat Mech, and Quantum Chemistry I/II.

Quantum chemistry *is* linear algebra. Hartree-Fock is a matrix eigenvalue
problem, the Roothaan equations are a generalized eigenvalue problem, and
basis-set work is change of basis. Szabo and Ostlund assumes fluency in vector
spaces, Hermitian operators, and diagonalization as structure rather than as
recipes, so the course has to deliver the conceptual layer and not a toolkit.

**Replaces MATH UN2015 (Linear Algebra and Probability), reversed 2026-11-06.**
The 2026-08-15 decision took UN2015 to save 3pt by bundling linear algebra with
probability. That is reversed for three reasons:

1. Columbia states plainly that UN2015 "does NOT replace MATH UN2010 as
   prerequisite requirements of math courses," and that a student cannot receive
   full credit for both. UN2015 is a one-way door — it unlocks nothing downstream
   and makes retaking UN2010 costly.
2. UN2010 lists linear transformations and canonical forms; UN2015 does not.
   That is exactly the operator-on-a-space layer quantum chemistry needs.
3. UN2015 is written for students who "wish to focus on applications and
   practical approaches," and is recommended for engineering, life sciences,
   social sciences, and economics. Chemical Physics is not on that list.

The probability content lost with UN2015 is real and still needed — stat mech
needs distributions, and molecular ML needs regression. That is better served by
a dedicated probability course later (STAT GU4203 is already on file as waived
and could be restored) than by UN2015's diluted half.
