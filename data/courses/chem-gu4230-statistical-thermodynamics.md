## Why This Course Matters

Statistical mechanics is the hinge of this entire plan. It is what connects the quantum
description of molecules to every macroscopic number a simulation predicts — free energies,
binding affinities, solubilities, phase behaviour. FEP+, the method that made Schrödinger,
is applied statistical mechanics. There is no route to building at the method level that
does not pass through this material.

Chosen over CHAP E4120 for four reasons:

- **Depth.** 4.5pt against 3pt, and the syllabus is the real object: classical *and*
  quantum statistical mechanics of gases, liquids and solids. Quantum statistical mechanics
  is precisely the bridge from the quantum chemistry sequence to condensed-phase behaviour.
- **Department.** Havemeyer, not chemical engineering. Taught by the chemistry faculty whose
  labs matter here, in the building where the research happens.
- **It completes a coherent triad.** GU4221 → GR6222 → GU4230 is one continuous chemistry
  theory core rather than three courses borrowed from three departments.
- **Prerequisites already line up.** Its corequisite is CHEM GU4221, taken in Semester V —
  satisfied well in advance. The stated prerequisite, elementary physical chemistry, is
  covered by CHEM UN3079/UN3080.

The computational-methods content that CHAP E4120 carried in its title is not lost: CHEM
GU4232 (Molecular Modeling) now covers implementation directly, and covers it better.

**Logistics** (from the most recent listing): Tu/Th 11:40am–12:55pm, 320 Havemeyer Hall,
in person, 4.5pt, standard grading. Fall term — matches Semester VII.
