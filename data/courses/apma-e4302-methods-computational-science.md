## Why This Course Matters

The only course on this schedule that treats *building and running scientific software* as
a subject in its own right. Its stated aim is getting students to the point where they can
run simulations on machines ranging from a single workstation to modern supercomputer
hardware: UNIX shells, version control, reproducibility, OpenMP, MPI, many-core.

The goal here is a platform, and a platform is not an algorithm — it is software other
people can run. Nothing else in four years addresses that.

It is also the right replacement for the COMS courses that were cut. Those would have
taught Java. This teaches C, Fortran, MPI and OpenMP, which is what computational chemistry
is actually written in — Gaussian is Fortran, the MD engines are C and CUDA.

**Prerequisite worth taking seriously:** APMA E4300 *and* working knowledge of C, Fortran
or a similar compiled language. There is no programming coursework anywhere in this plan,
so that has to come from self-study before Semester VIII. Unlike "learn machine learning,"
this is a bounded target with a fixed deadline: enough C to survive one course.

**Logistics:** 3pt. Confirm the term before counting on Semester VIII.
